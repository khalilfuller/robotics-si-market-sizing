Robotics Systems Integration Market Sizing

October 2026 · Khalil Fuller

Bottom line

The robotics, warehouse and manufacturing SI market today is about $55–65B of integration value-add on ~$110–120B of integrated project value. Robots moving into roughly ten new industries, such as clinical labs, hospitals, agriculture and construction, could take that to ~$135–170B by 2035, about 2.5x today.

The two methods agree within about 20% in both years. Read it as ~$60B today and ~$150B by 2035, growing about 10% a year.

Definitions and scope

  • Integration value-add, the headline metric: the SI's own revenue pool — engineering, software, controls, installation, commissioning and lifecycle service. It excludes the robot and any hardware the integrator passes through.

  • Integrated project value is what customers pay for turnkey systems, including resold hardware or, for OEM-integrators such as Daifuku, hardware they build themselves.

  • Scope: global, US$, calendar 2025 for today and nominal 2035 for the potential. Included: robot cells, warehouse and intralogistics systems, and controls/OT integration. Excluded: enterprise IT integration and surgical robots, which are sold direct.

Inputs:

  • Robot units and price: IFR counted 603,307 installs in 2025. Its record $16.7B install value on 542k units in 2024 implies about $30.8k per robot.

  • System multiple: BCG's 2015 cost build for a spot-welding cell in US automotive puts total system cost at 3.3x the robot in 2005, 4.0x in 2014 and 3.7x projected for 2025. Integrator guides cluster at 2–4x; IFR's 2012 study said 3x. The 3.0x here is the low end.

  • Value-add share: in the same build, systems engineering plus project management — the integrator's own labor, excluding hardware passed through — falls from 52% (2005) to 41% (2014) to 39% (2025 projected). Per project, engineering runs 20–45% and install 10–25%. The 45% here is above BCG.

  • Warehouse: the top-11 revenue sum comes from the company filings in the SI landscape table below (Daifuku $4.4B down to Swisslog $0.9B).

  • Controls: the CSIA counts ~2,200 integrators and "nearly $20B" of revenue, a c. 2006 figure that implies ~$9M per firm before growth.

  • Caveat: Daifuku's revenue includes cleanroom and auto-plant handling, and palletizing robots appear in both the IFR and warehouse numbers. Palletizing double-counts only slightly; Daifuku matters more, and the next section works it through.

The two errors offset. On BCG's 2025 structure — 3.7x at a 39% share — robot cells give $26.6B against the $25B above, 6% higher. BCG's projected $28k robot price brackets the $30.8k from IFR. Both are spot welding in US automotive; a global average including electronics, SCARA and cobot cells would carry less peripheral cost and less engineering.

Largest SIs, and how much of their revenue is SI

3. Bottoms-up: potential (2035)

2035 value-add runs $98B conservative, $137B medium and $193B on a fast takeoff.

Each row: universe × capex per site = the saturated TAM. A 2035 penetration gives the installed stock; deployment ramps linearly from 2025, so 2035 annual capex = stock ÷ 10. The SI tier turns that into value-add — A bespoke cells 45%, B mobile fleets 30%, C drop-in products 20%, anchored on Unchained's cost split. Universe counts are sourced; the penetrations are my judgment.

Reality check. Every medium case sits below its third-party forecast: agriculture $17.1B of capex against IMARC's $45.4B, restaurants $6.3B against Coherent's $8.4B, hospitals $3.5B against Market.us's $25.4B. The fast case passes the forecast only for restaurants and construction.

The ceiling. Saturate the six countable industries and integration alone is worth ~$43B a year, against $7.8B in the medium case. Agriculture is most of it: 5.7M farms over 50 ha at $250k each is a $1.4T installed base and $28.5B a year at full penetration, against $3.4B medium. Ten years does not close that gap.

One overlap stands. The humanoid row de-duplicates against the other new industries but not against the core segments: a humanoid on a warehouse or factory line sits in both. The $1.5–13.8B spread reflects forecast disagreement, not the overlap, which is unresolved.

Left out entirely: mining (Caterpillar and Komatsu integrate autonomy themselves), surgical robots (sold direct), retail micro-fulfillment (already inside the warehouse number), sidewalk delivery, recycling, laundry and defense.

4. Top-down: potential (2035)

Sizing from the labor robots can technically replace gives ~$167B of value-add in 2035 (range $78–332B).

  1. World GDP 2025 is $118.2T (IMF). At a 52.4% labour share (ILO, 2024), that is $61.9T of labor income.

  2. Robots could technically automate ~13% of US work hours (McKinsey Global Institute, Agents, robots, and us). That makes $8.05T of robot-addressable labor today, or $11.9T in 2035 at 4% nominal growth a year.

  3. Calibration: today's ~$91B of robot-cell and warehouse project spend replaces about 0.56% of that pool each year, assuming capex equals about two years of the labor it saves.

  4. 2035: the automation rate rises to 1.5% of the pool a year (range 1.0–2.2%). At 2.0 years of capex per $1 of labor (range 1.5–2.5), robot capex is $357B. A 40% SI share (range 35–45%) gives $143B, and non-robot controls integration growing at 6% adds $24B, for $167B.

Cross-check: McKinsey expects robotics hardware to reach $450B by 2040 — $80B industrial, $370B general-purpose. At 1.3x value-add per $1 of industrial robots and 0.3x for general-purpose ones, which need less integration by design, that is ~$215B of SI value-add in 2040, in line with $135–170B in 2035.

What would break this. MGI's 13% is technical potential, measured on US work hours. Physical work is a larger share of hours globally but is paid less. The unknown is pace: 0.56% a year today against the 1.5% assumed for 2035.

If it goes right

Integration becomes the toll road of the physical-AI economy. Robots take over the ~$11.9T of robot-addressable labor at 2–3% a year instead of 0.6%. Whoever turns deployment into a product — standard cells, AI-written controls, fleet service — earns a slice of every robot installed in every hospital, farm, kitchen and job site. That is a $300B+ annual pool by the mid-2030s with recurring service on top, and no company yet plays the Accenture or United Rentals role in it.

Confidence and limitations

Every figure above was checked against the source linked beside it.

Weakest inputs:

  • The system multiple and value-add share both rest on BCG's 2015 build for one application, spot welding in US automotive. Its 2025 column is a 2015 projection, not an observation.

  • Technavio's >20% services share cites an unnamed study.

  • Other firms put industrial automation lower — Straits at $209B for 2025 — so the low case could go lower still.

  • The 80M-worker construction subset and the top-11 warehouse sum of $24.2B are plausible but could not be traced to a single published page.

  • The fade rule, the tier B and C SI shares and the 50% humanoid de-duplication are judgment calls, not sourced figures.

  • Every 2035 penetration in Section 3 is my judgment. The universe counts are sourced; no survey forecasts what share of farms, restaurants or hospitals run robots in 2035, so the conservative-to-fast spread is an argument about adoption, not a measurement of it.

  • The $15B low case for humanoids comes from Interact Analysis via a secondary source and is not confirmed against the original.

  • Several 2035 rows lean on market-research CAGRs, which tend to run high.

The cheapest way to tighten this: 5–10 calls with integrators in hospitals, labs and agriculture, to price what their services share actually is.
